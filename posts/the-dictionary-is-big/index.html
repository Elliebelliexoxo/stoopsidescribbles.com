The dictionary is big.

November 25, 2026
I took a workout class recently and a song was playing that reeked of desperation. The instructor made a joke of it and said "she's just like me for real." I went back to that studio for a class a week later. The same song played. It was the same instructor and right on cue she delivered the same line. "she's just like me for real." In that moment, I had never felt less inspired. Maybe its a protective organ of sorts. A default. A lack of individual thinking. Too much time online or around others who are chronic social media users. Likely a culmination. Does that make my workout instructors sentiment inauthentic though? Or does it just illuminate another problem..... Online speech has become exponentially singular over the last couple of years. Too many of us are just-like-each-other-for-real. We are amidst a literary crisis. Now, instead of just writing "please, lets read more" or "delete Instagram" I want to share new ways for how can we can collectively improve our vocabulary. - Memorize a monologue, a poem, a song, a long toast. Just one body of well written work. Use those words when interacting. - Figurative language. Drawing from the mutual givens around us will exponentially help convey emotional depth. We need to promote the meaning of our words. - Spend some time in the rhyme zone. Just remembering words that rhyme. Do some alliteration. - Writing for fun and being thoughtful about using a dictionary. Look words up when you don't know them. Try to then use in conversation. This is no shame to my workout instructor. I'm sure there's a level of regimen that goes into her diction when teaching a structured, repetitive class. At the end of the day, I don't know her. That said, our words and our thoughts go hand in hand. When we lose our ability to speak clearly and pronounced, our thinking becomes vulnerable, susceptible to vague, surface-level conjunctions. Where do you sit on the bell curve? Where do we want to be?
