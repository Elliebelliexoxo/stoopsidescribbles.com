Taking professionalism personally

November 24, 2026
A week has 168 hours. I spend about 56 of those hours sleeping and 40 of those hours in the workplace. Last week, I had my one year anniversary in the corporate world. Today, I've begun my reflection on what has been truly internalized. How has my work routine enmeshed into my personality, livelihood, and authentic self? What I've found is an axiomatic layer of professionalism woven into my being. The corporate time commitment is a big one. I find its everyones biggest peeve about the system (myself included). This year, I’ve lost a lot of time I’d otherwise spend writing, practicing skills, and just experiencing the world. Creativity can feel somewhat fickle, or too effortful to explore after a full day of thinking. Working requires a specific type of cognition, practicality, and cerebral logic that, long term, might lead to a disconnectedness to ones spirit, or abstractness. To me, the soul lives in the crevasses of the mind that are less methodical. This part of me feels readily accessible but with some strain. I'm cautious and protective over my creativity. That said, there is a sense of maturity that my year working has brought me. To interact, daily, with people in a professional context has augmented my radar for privacy, disciplined my boundaries, and my honed my approach to commitment. Starting with privacy, relationships with colleagues add an air of appropriateness that previous environments (college and seasonal positions) have not. Operating within a setting that has an element of filtration can provide healthy distraction. It feels cool to be in a place where we are not talking about ourselves all the time, but rather the world we live in. At work, I've had great objective discussions on trends, rhetoric, the arts, and places around the world. I've learned many new facts and have heard awesome stories from people who I might not have ever interacted with in my own social environments. Alls this to say, the workplace is a great setting to practice boundaries, and learn random things. Additionally, the corporate world has been a true crash course in commitment. Not just to a job, but to a team. There is something inspiring and simple about teamwork. It is an important life lesson to know how to foster it and how to show up for your team, for a goal bigger than yourself, every day, even when we don't feel like it. The reality of this day and age is that the world is that we are living in is saturated, stimulated and supercharged. Because of this, the routine and etiquette of an office might, for some, feel sterile, robotic, or bland when juxtaposed. For others, it might bring comfort, steadiness, and a much needed relief from the busyness of society. None of this is a revelatory take, but its new insight I'm starting to really understand now. All in all, professionalism has its flaws but I know now that it can also be a wonderful and comforting thing to explore. I'm sure part of my changes from this last year are due to pure human development.The nuances that make up a person accumulate from a host of places. I'm grateful from what I've gathered thus far in this chapter of my life.
