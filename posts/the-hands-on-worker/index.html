The Hands on Worker

July 24, 2026
After two decades of an increasingly digital livelihood, I have a speculation that our physical world is now in ascendance. My theory is not rooted in some belief that we are all aware enough to ban together, decide enough is enough, and put our devices away. I unfortunately feel as though we are past that, and too distracted to collectively draw ourselves out of the pit we have fallen into. That said, I believe the return to the physical world is coming, and that, for many, it will be obligatory because of to work and the landscape of the job market. To begin, here are some things I keep seeing show up: 1. Unemployment in the US is high 2. White-collar jobs are becoming increasingly automated 3. Blue-collar, trades, and agricultural jobs are less vulnerable to AI since they are more labor intensive. 4. Teenagers are having trouble finding beginner work experience and are being told to look in unconventional places (A Good Morning America broadcast I saw recently suggested they look at car washing, for example) The current landscape of the job market points me to believe that more teenagers will seek agricultural, physical, and mechanical jobs due to the current landscape of what is accessible, available, and less likely to be automated. Like I said earlier, I believe the return to the physical world will be largely obligatory due to work. Society is very focused on digital AI, but I imagine there will be a shift towards robotics and Physical AI in the impending future. I believe it is already happening. Which brings me to mentioning new US government regulations. First, some helpful context - the FCC’s Covered List is of equipment and services that are deemed to "pose an unacceptable risk to the national security of the U.S. or the safety and security of U.S. persons." On July 28 the FCC added 'foreign-produced power inverters and foreign-produced advanced robotic devices' to this list. For robots in the US this means: 1. The robot must be manufactured in the United States. 2. The robot must Pass the Domestic Content Test: The cost of domestic components must exceed 65% of the total cost of all components (rising to 75% in 2029). Learning this, I immediately thought "this is where the jobs will go. People will begin working in robotics." If you are curious as to how/if robots are reaaally going to be a thing, here's some nice-to-knows about the leading AI companies in the US: Although Anthropic (Claude) does not manufacture or build physical robots, the company actively researches how its models can control, program, and operate robotic hardware. Likewise, OpenAI (ChatGPT) is actively developing in-house robotics hardware and data-collection labs. All in all, I imagine the 2030s will bring about a lot of physicality. Tinkering. Fiddling. Tweaking. Mucking about. Hopefully we can have fun with these imagined robots and make them do our dishes. Less doom and gloom.
